Foreword: May 22nd

Hi Nicole,

I'm not sure if you heard, but Canada Post employees intend to go on strike tomorrow, May 23rd.

"If that happens, no new mail will be accepted and any items already in the system will be held until the strike is over. " -CBC

I wrote you a letter on May 16th which my father posted for me the following morning. I wonder where it will end up. With that in mind, and with everything else, I thought I would send over a little digital communiqué. I just want to care for you from afar, in a way that makes sense for you and respects your space. Please do let me know if you feel as though the format doesn't suit you. Here it goes.


Communiqué #1 - May 22nd

Dearest Nicole,
I began writing this on the toilet at Vanier. I felt a spark of joy in my chest after finishing my class. This joy brought your face into my mind's eye - right in front. It reminded me of the fountain of joy you spring into my life, for which I am immensly grateful. That spark inspired me to share my joy with you. Teaching is going well, I think. I recieved a bag of Armenian chocolates last night after the exam and a chewy bar, (or something of that ilk) this morning. Now I'm in my living room, musical instruments scattered about on the floor, and I may very well dip, soon, into that bag of Armenian goodies...

You mentioned that the Geese have landed. I hope they are great in number and healthy, and that it is a bountiful hunting season for the Arviarmiut. That photo of you in your recently-furred parka, holding a goose by the head, is to be framed and hung on every doorpost, electrical pole, and mailbox in Beaconsfield, Pierrefonds, and Roxboro. It is, truly, a photo for the ages. You look joyous and powerful. And, dare I say, slightly goose-like. I hope your work is going well. Is the food photographer mixing well with everyone else? 50 Geese killed two days ago, you say? I can barely imagine the beauty in your surroundings. But also, I imagine, the harshness.

There is destruction everywhere. The city came a-knockin' this morning to cut the tree in front of my apartment. I had a feeling it would be coming soon - orange spray paint marking it for death - but I didn't think it would be as soon as now. Construction all over the city, and, would you believe, Cheskie's is opening a new store front and closing down the old one.
Things are changing so fast.

I'll keep these short as I'm sure you are so busy and I don't want to be a burden. I'm sending you all my love. I'm sitting, now, with your absence and the sadness it brings me. If this short blurb brought you a small drop of joy - or cracked a smile - then I will be so content.
Love,
jo

marked for death
naked and bare
out with old, in with new
